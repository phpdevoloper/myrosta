# Myrosta — UI refinement strategy · 8 Sep 2026

**Verdict.** The engine is good and the visual bones are good; the growth of the last eight months was added one card, one button and one colour at a time on top of a layout designed for one screen and one staff group. The consequences are concentrated in three places: the phone header, the admin settings page, and a type-and-colour system that drifted. None of it needs a redesign — it needs an order, a floor, and a spine.

**Method.** Rendered the current live build with fictional data on desktop and at phone size; measured every screen (heights, tap targets, text sizes, colours, tokens). Six independent reviewers then examined the code and those measurements from six angles — navigation, staff on a phone, the admin workflow, the visual system, wording, accessibility — and a skeptic tried to refute each reviewer’s top two findings. Twelve verifications: none refuted, none already done; ten “partly” (real, but the reviewer’s proposal was bigger than needed) and two confirmed outright. Fifty-eight findings in total, all cited to the line in the code, kept in the appendix.

## What we measured

- **302 of 812 px** — the header on a phone before any content — 12 tabs wrapping into three rows; a third of the screen
- **7th of 9** — where “My shifts”, the screen staff open the app for, sits in the staff tab row
- **5,912 px** — the length of My shifts on a phone; 33 of its 33 tap targets are under the 44 px minimum, 24 text runs are under 12 px
- **16 cards · 131 inputs** — Admin settings on one page — 6,625 px on desktop, 12,696 px on a phone, 71 of 72 targets under 44 px
- **15,339 px** — the roster on a phone: one card per day, 60 identical “Open this day to assign” buttons, no “today”
- **16 sizes, 8–25 px** — distinct font sizes in use; there is no type scale
- **35 + 33** — hard-coded colours in the stylesheet and the script, beside 40 perfectly good design tokens
- **2.6 : 1 and 3.0 : 1** — contrast of hint text and of white-on-teal buttons; accessibility needs 4.5 : 1
- **87 · 0** — toasts that carry every message including errors, none announced to screen readers · roster cells reachable by keyboard
- **2 live bugs** — Equity bars are invisible (a colour token that was never defined) and a second undefined token in the roster grid

## The plan — six phases, each shippable on its own

### The safe CSS pass · This week · ½–1 day — **DONE 8 Sep 2026** (commit on main; verified by three skeptics, their eleven confirmed/likely gaps folded in: tinted-header day names, zone buttons, badges, archive note, tablet wrap instead of scroll, focus-ring room, checkbox rows, static live regions, brand-hover, lint tightened)

Everything here is a token or a rule, ships in one commit, and every phone user feels it the same afternoon.

Ships:
- Equity bars visible again, and the second undefined token fixed
- Hint text and primary buttons brought to AA contrast with a text-safe teal (the brand teal stays for rings, bars and the logo)
- A 44 px minimum on every control under 640 px
- The tab row scrolls sideways instead of wrapping — the phone header drops from 302 px to roughly 150 px before anything else is built
- Toasts announced to screen readers; errors interrupt, status messages don’t

Risk: None to the engine — stylesheet and three attributes

### Phone-first navigation and the staff home · Before 1 October · ≈ 1½ weeks — **DONE 8 Sep 2026** (branch ui-phase1 → main; the desktop account menu and the separate open-shift strip were deferred: desktop keeps View as / Sign out in the bar, and the open-shift count sits in the action row)

Site 2’s nurses, junior doctors, scribes and clerks will meet Myrosta on their phones first. Their first ten seconds should answer “when am I on next, and what can I do about it”.

Ships:
- A bottom bar on phones — staff: My shifts · Book shifts · Requests · More; admin: Roster · Round · Requests · Settings · More
- Tabs ordered by role and renamed by job: Book shifts, Day board, Leave, Fairness, Settings, Audit log — the word “booking” used the same way in tabs, banners and notifications

- My shifts rebuilt by urgency: the next-shift card with an action row (Book · Request leave or swap · Open shifts · Calendar), then this week, then a one-line quota strip; the whole cycle, the statistics and the export controls fold away until wanted
- Open shifts surface under the next-shift card when there are any, with a 44 px Claim
- Notifications become a sheet whose rows take you to the shift, the request or the round they mention

Risk: Presentation only; wired tab by tab; engine and tests untouched

### The admin’s working order · October · ≈ 1½ weeks — **DONE 8 Sep 2026** (branch ui-phase2 → main, 203/203; three skeptics, twenty-odd confirmed items folded in: status verbs, the skipped fixed step, the rolling schedule, archived cycles, the evidence-based checklist)

Ships:

Risk: Low — the status logic is already a pure function; its buttons just render in a second place

### One visual system · October – November, incrementally · ≈ 1 week — **DONE 8 Sep 2026** (branch ui-phase3 → main, 231/231 + seven lints; three skeptics, fourteen confirmed items fixed — contrast of the rail, red counts and hairlines, PH hue, a dangling `col` in Save group details, lint holes; lints: token, font-size, hex, PALETTE mirror, retired classes, button emoji, icon; measured: 126 → 0 px literals, 32 → 0 stylesheet hex, 29 → 0 emoji buttons, 99 old labels → 100 tags)

The palette and type drifted because there was no scale to stay on. Naming what already works turns forty tokens into the whole system, and the next feature lands on it instead of beside it.

Ships:
- A seven-step type scale in relative units, so text respects the phone’s text-size setting; the sixteen sizes map onto it
- Every colour named: the seven leave colours, one neutral tint, one red; public holidays stop borrowing the PM amber; roughly seventy literals disappear
- One “tag” component replacing nine slightly different small labels
- Proper icons in buttons instead of emoji, which render differently on every device
- The brand decision (below) applied consistently
- The signature: the period colours — AM blue, PM amber, CST green — carried into the next-shift card and the app icon, so the first thing on the phone speaks the roster’s language
- A token check added to the test suite so an undefined colour can never ship again

Risk: Visual regressions only — done component by component with before/after screenshots

### Usable by keyboard, by screen reader, and at 2 am · October – November, alongside phase 3 · ≈ 1 week — **DONE 8 Sep 2026** (branch ui-phase4 → main, 309/309 + 7 lints; three skeptics, 26 confirmed items fixed — focus now survives every render, every toggle announces its state, no nested labels)

A roster clerk on a keyboard cannot reach a single roster cell today, and a consultant with reading glasses off cannot make the text bigger. Both are fixable without touching how anything works.

Ships:
- Roster cells, staff rows and planner days become real buttons — reachable by keyboard, named for screen readers
- One dialog helper for the six modals: focus moves in, the page behind is inert, focus returns on close
- Errors shown beside the field they belong to, not only as a toast
- Labels attached to the 131 settings inputs and the header selectors
- Meaning never by colour alone: a visible note mark on edited shifts, “AM 3/4 short” in words
- The phone roster grouped by week, current week open, a Today chip

Risk: Low — mechanical changes to markup

## Decisions needed

- **Start phase 0 now?** Recommended: yes. It is a day, it is safe, and it is the fastest visible improvement on every phone.
- **A bottom bar on phones, and the tab renames?** Recommended: yes to both. “Book shifts”, “Day board”, “Leave”, “Fairness”, “Settings”, “Audit log” — names by job, not by feature.
- **Brand: one Myrosta teal for every group, or a colour per group?** Recommended: one teal. Identity comes from the hospital and group name in the header. Per-group colour today recolours only two elements and would need re-testing for contrast per group.
- **Make CST a per-group option (hidden for groups that don’t have it)?** Recommended: yes — it is a consultant contract term; a nurse or scribe should never see a CST button.
- **The booking sheet before the 23 November round?** Recommended: yes, and rehearse it with two consultants in early November.
- **Dark mode?** Later, opt-in, after phase 3.

## Protect — do not lose these

- The next-shift card — one dark surface, an eyebrow, the shift in bold, a quota bar; it is already the app’s signature
- The AM / PM / CST / leave colour language and the roster grid’s mechanics: sticky name column, cover row, red-amber-green
- The capacity check: every line of the arithmetic shown, verdicts in plain words
- The confirmation texts for archiving, deleting and removing — they name the object and the consequence
- One source of truth for “can this person book now” and its plain sentence for staff
- The bones already in the stylesheet: visible focus ring, reduced motion respected, 16 px inputs so iPhones don’t zoom, safe-area insets
- The phone roster degrading to day cards instead of a squeezed grid
- The open-round banner — the app’s one true deep link

## Appendix — every finding, by area (line numbers refer to app.html on 8 Sep 2026)

### Navigation and structure

Myrosta's navigation is a single admin-ordered tab array (TABS, app.html:1393) rendered as a wrapping row of 9–12 pill buttons inside a header that also holds the cycle selector, status pill, cloud pill, bell, View-as/Signed-in, Switch group and Sign out (app.html:336–344, render() 1457–1481). That worked for one org of 30 consultants eight months ago; it now costs a phone user 302px (37% of the screen) before content, puts the staff member's primary screen "My shifts" 7th, and gives admins no single place to "run the round" or "fix gaps" — those jobs are spread across Self-roster, Admin settings → Roster status and the Roster grid, joined only by prose ("Admin settings → Roster status" appears 3× and there are zero in-content tab links outside render()). The fix is structural but contained: split the one TABS list into a role-ordered primary set plus a "More" group, render it as a bottom nav on phones and a top strip on desktop, add a tiny go(tab, cycle, anchor) helper so every prose cross-reference becomes a link, and consolidate the round/gap controls onto the screens where the work happens. None of this touches the engine (tests/run.py stays green); the risk is in wiring (per-tab handlers inside wire() are gated by S.tab) and can be staged tab by tab.

### Wording

Myrosta's copy is unusually thoughtful for an 8-month-old single-file app: the cycle confirm() dialogs, the capacity-check prose and the emptyState() helper are better than most commercial rostering tools. The problems are the ones that come from growth: the publish flow has three verb families for one state machine ("Release / Open / Publish (go live) / Unpublish / Take offline / Back to draft") and the header pill disagrees with the settings card for rolling cycles; the staff-facing screens still speak ED-consultant contract language (CST, UA, EFT, ROC+OC, quota) as bare button labels that a nurse or scribe at site 2 will not decode; eight admin strings still hard-code "consultant" despite the ROLE() helpers; the four self-roster methods are named after algorithms (cohort waves, snake-draft, guarded free-for-all) rather than what people experience; the booking-guard reasons are lower-case fragments with ISO dates that get toasted raw; and publishing notifies the whole department with no confirm and no toast. All findings are label/string edits or a small modal helper — none touch engine functions, but three test assertions in tests/suite.js (lines 46, 232, 236) regex on reason substrings "quota", "isn't live yet" and "passed", so those words must survive any rewrite.

Proposed style guide (8 rules):
1. A button names the outcome the person controls — verb + object ("Publish roster", "Send leave request", "Save shift times"); never a bare "Submit", "Apply", "Request" or "OK".
2. One verb per flow, and the state label echoes it: "Open self-roster" produces "Self-roster open"; "Publish roster" produces "Published"; "Go live" produces "Live". The header pill, the cycle selector, the settings card and the notification all use the same word.
3. Expand a code the first time it appears on a screen — "CST (clinical support time)", "UA (unavailable)", "EFT (full-time fraction)" — then abbreviate; a code is never a button label on its own.
4. Never hard-code a role noun or an ED example in a string: use ROLE()/ROLES()/ROLEC(), and let placeholders and zone examples come from branding, not Werribee.
5. Every blocking message is a full second-person sentence that ends with what to do next, and dates are formatted with fmtISO ("Sat 3 Oct"), never "2026-10-03".
6. A destructive or broadcast action confirms with the named object, the consequence, and the reversibility — and the confirm button repeats the verb ("Delete cycle Oct 2026", "Publish and notify 16 people"), not OK/Cancel.
7. Sentence case everywhere (tiles, pills, headings, table headers); no emoji or arrows inside labels — icons come from the existing TIC/EIC SVG set or nothing; "✓" only marks a selected state.
8. The sub-line under a card heading is one sentence saying what the card controls and its scope ("This cycle only"); multi-step workflows become a stepped checklist with state (the setupGuideHTML step() pattern), never a sentence with ① → ② → ③.

### Accessibility and robustness

The stylesheet already has the right foundations (global :focus-visible ring at app.html:218, reduced-motion kill switch at :264, iOS zoom-guard and safe-area insets in the phone media block at :296–331, chip labels that carry text) but the interaction layer built on top of it is mouse-and-sighted-only. The roster clerk on a keyboard cannot reach a single roster cell, staff row or planner day (all are <td>/<div> with onclick and no tabindex), every modal is a bare innerHTML overlay with no dialog role, focus move or trap, and the only feedback channel is an un-announced toast (87 call sites) that also carries validation errors.
