prompt pack is written only after the script is approved. Decisions taken with the founder on
6 October: a braided story (the rota lead and one staff member across one cycle); three moments (the
round opens on every phone, a swap when life happens, the director's glance); stills first as the
approval gate, then the same frames animated into a master and its cutdowns; a fictional hospital
emergency department, with the whole-department board in the director's glance.*

## Rules this film keeps

1. **Every app screen is a real recording** of Myrosta in its demo sandbox, with the sandbox's
   synthetic people and the hospital name set to "Wattle Hospital" for the film. Nothing on screen is
   drawn by AI. No real roster, person, hospital or group ever appears.
2. **No claim beyond the product.** Staff book up to a fair share the rota lead sets; a swap is agreed
   by the two people and approved by the rota lead; the director reads what the app shows. Myrosta
   holds no patient data by design and is hosted in Sydney. Nothing about pay rates, savings or
   outcomes is claimed.
3. **A film about staff, not care.** Patients are never identifiable: background blur, a curtain, a
   trolley from behind. No monitor numbers, no drugs, no clinical instruction of any kind.
4. **Nothing real is borrowed.** No hospital names, logos, state insignia, uniforms with patches or real
   faces. The school that rings is never named. Phone numbers, where glimpsed, are from ACMA's range
   reserved for creative works.
5. **Nothing readable in a generated frame.** Every readable screen is a recording cut in full-frame.
   Phones in generated shots face away or show a soft glow.
6. **The narrator is an AI voice and never speaks as a real person.** No "I" lines, no cloned or
   imitated voice: a stock voice from the generator's library.
7. **The end card discloses it:** dramatisation, fictional people and hospital, scenes and voices made
   with AI, app screens real recordings of Myrosta.

## Master script (about 2 min 30 s, as approved; the film as cut follows the order in "Changes since approval")

### Scene 1 · Sunday, 11:10 pm — the spreadsheet night (0:00–0:25)
**Picture.** A kitchen at night, one lamp. Sam in a grey hoodie at the table, a laptop open on a vast
grid, columns running off the screen; a mug gone cold, a printed sheet with pencil crossings. His
phone buzzes face up beside the laptop, then buzzes again. Cut to another kitchen, morning light: a
roster printed and pinned to a fridge under a school magnet; Emma, in scrubs, runs a finger along a
row while a child's voice asks something from the next room.
**Insert.** A mock spreadsheet made for the film: a huge grid, cells unreadable, a scroll that never
reaches the end.
**Sam** (to nobody, reading the phone): "That's three swaps since dinner."
**Emma** (calling through the doorway): "Saturday, love. It's on the fridge."
**VO:** "Eleven at night, and the roster is a spreadsheet on a kitchen table. Every swap is a text,
every leave request an email, and the only copy that matters is on one laptop."

### Scene 2 · Monday, 8:05 am — the round opens (0:25–0:50)
**Picture.** Morning handover at the nurses' station: a dozen staff, phones buzzing in pockets almost
together; a few glance down and smile. Sam, in teal scrubs, puts his own phone away. Cut to Emma at a
café window with a flat white, thumb moving on her phone, a small nod as something fills. Cut to Sam
at the doctors' station, laptop open, the grid on screen filling in while he drinks his coffee.
**Insert (real).** Book shifts on the phone: the day sheet, zone chips reading *Open*, two taps, the
confirm, and the receipt line "That's your share — 8 of 8 booked." Then the Roster tab on the laptop,
"Booking open", the grid filling across a morning.
**Emma** (half to herself): "Resus Tuesday… and the Thursday evening. Done."
**Sam** (glancing at the laptop): "Half full already."
**VO:** "With Myrosta, the rota lead opens the round, and the roster lands on every phone at once.
Each person books their own shifts, up to a fair share, and the grid fills itself."

### Scene 3 · Two weeks later, Thursday, 1:40 pm — life happens (0:50–1:25)
**Picture.** A corridor off the department; Emma steps out of the flow with her phone to her ear,
listening, then exhales. She looks at the phone, thumb moving: two taps and a short pause. Cut to the
staff car park: Alex beside his bike, cycling jacket half on, phone in hand; he reads, grins, taps
once. Cut to Emma's phone lighting up in her hand. Cut to Sam walking the corridor between two
rooms, phone up, one tap without breaking stride. Emma, already shrugging on the denim jacket,
heads for the doors.
**Insert (real).** My shifts on the phone → the Saturday shift → *Swap it* → *Ask everyone*. On
Alex's phone, *Swaps wanted*: Emma's Saturday, and his offer of a shift back. On Emma's phone the
offer arrives; she accepts. On Sam's phone, Requests: the pending swap, tagged "via Ask everyone",
the two shifts side by side, *Approve*. Emma's phone: the swap is confirmed.
**Emma** (on the phone, gently): "I'll come and get her. Twenty minutes."
**Alex** (in the car park, to himself): "I'll take your Saturday."
**Sam** (in the corridor): "Cover's fine. Approved."
**VO:** "Two weeks later, life happens. From the shift itself, Emma asks the whole group. Alex offers
a shift back; she accepts; and the rota lead approves it in a minute, with cover and fairness in
front of him. Nobody rings around."

### Scene 4 · Friday, 8:50 am — the director's glance (1:25–1:50)
**Picture.** Morgan's office, blinds half open, a meeting folder under one hand, a laptop under the
other. She reads, scrolls, tilts her head. Sam leans in at the door with two coffees.
**Insert (real).** The Day board for today, cover by zone. The switch to *Whole department*: five
rosters under one heading, Wattle Hospital, consultants, nurses, junior doctors, scribes and admin.
The Fairness tab: who is carrying the tough shifts. The Audit log: every change, by whom, when.
**Morgan** (to Sam, not looking up): "So that's everyone."
**Sam:** "Five rosters. One heading."
**VO:** "The director sees what the spreadsheet never showed: today's cover, every roster in the
department under one heading, who is carrying the tough shifts, and every change written down.
Myrosta holds no patient data, and it's hosted in Sydney."

### Scene 5 · Sunday, 6:40 pm — Sam's evening (1:50–2:05)
**Picture.** A back garden at dusk; a boy of about ten, seen from behind, kicks a ball to Sam in a
faded polo. Sam's phone lies face down on the outdoor table. It lights up once; he turns it over,
one tap, puts it down, kicks the ball back.
**Insert (real).** On Sam's phone: the Round tab, the round closed, one tap, "Published (live)".
Then a staff phone, a notification arriving: the roster is live.
**Sam** (to his son): "No, I'm done. Round's closed."
**VO:** "And the rota lead gets the evening back."

### Scene 6 · Monday, 7:20 am — close (2:05–2:30)
**Picture.** First light at the staff entrance; Emma and Sam arrive from different directions and
walk in together, phones in pockets, talking about something else. The doors slide open on the
department's morning.
**VO:** "Myrosta was built in an emergency department by an emergency doctor, because a roster
should live on the phone in your pocket, be fair to the people on it, and take minutes, not nights.
One department. One eight-week pilot."
**End card.** Myrosta wordmark · myrosta.com.au. Small print: "Dramatisation. Characters and
hospital are fictional. Scenes and voices created with AI; app screens are real recordings of
Myrosta."
