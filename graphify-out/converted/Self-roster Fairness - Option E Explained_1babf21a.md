Self-roster fairness — Option E explained
Drafted for you · how it works in detail
In one sentence: You tell the system your preferences once; it drafts the entire roster in a single pass, balancing everyone's wishes against the fairness record; you review, swap and publish.
The timeline each cycle
1.  Preference week — mark your days exactly as in Option C: AM/PM likes, unlimited “prefer not” days, and capped “cannot work” (UA) days with the same visible budget and zone-aware coverage floor — plus standing preferences such as “prefer mornings”, “cluster my shifts together” or “spread them out”.
2.  Window closes — no more changes.
3.  Draft day — the engine builds the whole roster for everyone at once.
4.  Draft published with explanations — you see what you got and why; review and swap window follows.
5.  Roster published
How the engine decides
1.  Hard rules first — capped UA days, leave, staffing numbers and safe-hours rules are never broken — they are walls, not preferences.
2.  Preference score — the draft aims to give each person the highest possible share of their marked days — avoiding prefer-not days wherever it can — with everyone weighted equally.
3.  Contested days go to the ledger — when more people want a day than there are slots, two observable facts decide — no scores, no opinions: who has missed the most marked days recently (the same carry-over idea as Option C's priority points), and who carries the higher tough-shift share. The others' ledgers are credited — compensation is built into the engine.
4.  Everything is explained — each person sees a summary like “you received 9 of your 11 marked days; Saturday 15th went to a colleague who had missed more marked days recently.”
Worked example
Saturday 15th, PM, 2 slots — Alex, Sam, Kim and Jo all marked it. The engine consults the ledger: Jo missed the most marked days last cycle, and Kim currently carries the highest tough-shift share — they get the two slots. Alex and Sam are credited, which puts them first in line at the next contested day they've marked.
The edge rules, stated once
- Quotas don't change — the engine fills your usual EFT-based number of shifts, never more.
- One clinical period per day — never both, and never on a UA day.
- Zones — balanced automatically across the cycle.
- After the draft — swaps work exactly as now — the draft is a starting point, not a verdict.
Note: colleagues on fixed patterns are unaffected under this option — their shifts are entered first, exactly as now, and the process above applies only to flexible staff, over the slots that remain.
The takeaway
This option asks the least of you — mark your days once and receive a roster that has weighed every trade-off at once. What you give up is watching the decision happen: even with clear explanations, the deciding lives inside an algorithm, and “the computer chose” sits more comfortably with some people than others. The input is easy; the output takes trust.
